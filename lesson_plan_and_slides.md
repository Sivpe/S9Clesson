# Prepare B2 (Level S9C) - Unit 20: Places to Hang Out & A Place of Your Own
## Master Teacher Lesson Plan & Pedagogical Guide

**Target Level:** CEFR B2 / Secondary S9C  
**Unit Topic:** Places to Hang Out / Youth Spaces / DIY Architecture & Autonomy  
**Lesson Duration:** 60–90 minutes (Modular)  
**Main Materials:** Prepare B2 Student's Book Unit 20 (pp. 112–113), Interactive Slide Deck, 100-Point Vocabulary Worksheet, 100-Point Reading Worksheet.

---

## 1. Lesson Objectives & Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Vocabulary (Lexical Resource):**
   - Accurately identify and produce 15 core architectural and hangout terms (*arts complex, basement, concrete, surface, barriers, chimney, community centre, hut, steps, frame, shelter, shed, ladder, bench, rug*).
   - Distinguish subtle nuances between easily confused shelter types (*hut* vs. *shed* vs. *shelter* vs. *community centre*).
   - Use dynamic B2 phrasal verbs and idioms related to construction and renovation (*do up, pull down, chill out, hunt for, take someone to court*).
2. **Reading (Reading for Detail, Inferences & Inverted Questioning):**
   - Skim and scan two contrasting texts about unconventional youth building projects.
   - Infer speaker attitude, legal/safety constraints, and motivations.
   - Master the advanced reading skill of **Inverted Text-to-Question Formulation** (identifying which interview question matches a speaker's answer based on context clues).
3. **Speaking & Critical Thinking (Productive Skills):**
   - Evaluate community youth provisions and propose ideal hanging-out spaces using persuasive B2 structures (conditionals, modal deductions).

## 2. Detailed Stage-by-Stage Lesson Plan

### Stage 1: Warm-Up & Lead-In (8–10 mins)
* **Objective:** Activate schema about teenage social spaces and spark emotional engagement.
* **Teacher Script:**  
  *"Good morning everyone! Look at the top of page 112. Think about your favourite place to relax on a Friday evening. Is it outdoors, at a friend's house, a café, or somewhere private? Turn to your partner and answer: 'What is the one thing your ideal hangout MUST have?'"*
* **Interactive Class Poll / Boardwork:**
  - *Where do you hang out?* (Street, mall, skatepark, room, park)
  - *Problem:* Why do teenagers often feel there are "no good places for us" in modern cities? (Noise complaints, commercialisation, lack of public benches/parks).
* **Photo Elicitation (Photos A, B, C on p. 112):**
  - Ask: *"What do you notice about these three locations? Which looks the most adventurous? Which looks the most relaxing?"*
  - Photo A: High wooden ladder / treehouse.
  - Photo B: Rustic, vintage wooden hut / shed.
  - Photo C: Urban skatepark / concrete surface with street art.

---

### Stage 2: Vocabulary Discovery & Deepening (15–20 mins)
* **Objective:** Introduce and contextualise the 15 key vocabulary items; clarify subtle semantic boundaries.
* **Step 1: Contextual Cloze (SB p. 112 Ex 2)**
  - Students read the three paragraphs and place words from the three boxes.
  - *Teacher Strategy:* Don't just give answers. Ask Concept Checking Questions (CCQs).
  
  **Paragraph 1: Urban Cultural & Skate Spot**
  - *Target Words:* `complex`, `basement`, `concrete`, `surface`, `barriers`
  - *Key CCQ:* *"Is the Southbank Centre just one cinema or a cluster of cultural venues?"* -> A **complex** (a collection of connected buildings).
  - *Key CCQ:* *"Why is concrete ideal for skateboarding?"* -> It provides a smooth, durable **surface**.
  - *Key CCQ:* *"What do metal barriers do?"* -> Separate areas or act as obstacles/grind rails.

  **Paragraph 2: Renovated Historic Youth Spot**
  - *Target Words:* `hut`, `community centre`, `chimney`, `steps`
  - *Key CCQ:* *"If a building was 'done up', was it destroyed or renovated?"* -> Renovated / redecorated!
  - *Key CCQ:* *"Why does the hut need a chimney?"* -> To vent smoke so they can light an open fire safely.

  **Paragraph 3: Hand-Built Treehouse**
  - *Target Words:* `frame`, `shelter`, `shed`, `ladder`, `bench`, `rug`
  - *Key CCQ:* *"What is the 'frame' of a house?"* -> The skeleton/structural skeleton of timber or metal.
  - *Key CCQ:* *"What's the difference between a shed and a shelter?"*
    - **Shed:** An enclosed small building, usually in a garden, primarily for storing tools/equipment.
    - **Shelter:** Any structure providing basic cover/protection from bad weather (rain/wind/sun).
    - **Hut:** A small, crude or basic single-room shelter/dwelling.

* **Step 2: Semantic Distinction Matrix (SB p. 112 Ex 3)**
  - Guide students through categorising:
    1. *Buildings:* Hut, shed, community centre, (arts) complex.
    2. *Non-building structural items & how we use them:*
       - **Ladder / Steps:** To ascend/descend vertically.
       - **Bench:** Long seat for multiple people.
       - **Rug:** Fabric floor covering for warmth and comfort.
       - **Frame:** Support structure.
       - **Chimney:** Ventilation for smoke from a fireplace.
       - **Surface:** The top or outer boundary of something.
       - **Barriers:** Safety railings or obstacles.
       - **Basement:** The sub-ground architectural level.
    3. *Types of complexes:* Sports complex, shopping complex, cinema complex, residential/housing complex.

### Stage 3: Pre-Reading & Gist Reading (10 mins)
* **Objective:** Teach top-down reading strategies (skimming first sentences, predicting narrative arcs).
* **Task 1: First Line Prediction (SB p. 113 Ex 1)**
  - Text 1 first sentence: *"An award-winning tree house, which a group of boys spent three years building, will be pulled down over health and safety fears."*
    - *Question:* What will happen? Why?
    - *Answer:* It will be demolished/dismantled because the landowner fears someone might get injured and take him to court.
  - Text 2 first sentence: *"It was the 'Small House Movement' that inspired Celina Dill's project of building a tiny home on wheels for herself."*
    - *Question:* What is she building? Why?
    - *Answer:* A self-sufficient tiny house on wheels so she can travel anywhere with complete independence.
* **Task 2: Compare & Contrast (Ex 1 Q3 & Ex 2)**
  - *Similarities:* Both constructed by teenagers (Dougie 16, Celina 16), both hand-built using woodworking/practical craft skills, both represent a desire for personal autonomy and creative freedom.
  - *Differences:*
    - *Dougie & Friends:* Fixed location (in a tree on someone else's farmland), collaborative group project, recreational hangout, facing demolition due to liability.
    - *Celina Dill:* Mobile (on wheels/trailer), solo project, permanent future home, legally compliant and independent.

### Stage 4: In-Depth Reading & Inverted Question Formulation (15–20 mins)
* **Objective:** Develop high-level B2 text-matching and question-generation skills (SB p. 113 Ex 3).
* **Pedagogical Guide:**
  In this textbook activity, students are given an interviewee's quote and must (a) find the sentence in the text that provides the factual basis, and (b) formulate what the interviewer asked.

| # | Speaker Quote | Text Evidence (Paragraph / Line) | Suggested Interviewer Question |
|---|---|---|---|

| **2** | *"It's impossible to remove it because of how the tree has grown."* | *"Apparently, the construction is now so wrapped in branches that the whole tree will have to be cut down."* (Story 1, Para 3) | *Why can't you just dismantle the tree house without harming the tree?* |

### Stage 5: Productive Speaking & Extension Debate (10–15 mins)
* **Talking Points Discussion (SB p. 113):**
  1. *What kind of hangouts do young people in our area need most?*
  2. *What are the real pros and cons of having a mobile hangout on wheels vs. a permanent clubhouse?*
* **Collaborative Design Challenge:** In pairs, design a 100% sustainable teen hangout using at least 5 target words. Pitch it to the class in 60 seconds!

---

### Stage 6: Assessment & Consolidation (Homework)
* Distribute or assign the **100-Point Vocabulary Worksheet** and **100-Point Reading Worksheet**.
* Provide formative feedback on student responses using the rubric provided.
